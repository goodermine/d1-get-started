VOX can only work with what you give it. A noisy, clipped, half-room-echo recording produces murky metrics — and murky metrics produce vague practice. Five habits fix most of it.

## 1. Get close, but not too close

Around 15–20 cm from the microphone is a good starting zone for most phones and USB mics. Close enough to beat the room, far enough to avoid plosives slamming the capsule.

## 2. Watch the peak level

If the loudest moment of your take is hitting the ceiling, the recording clips and the analysis loses information. Aim to keep peaks comfortably below maximum — VOX will tell you if a take clipped.

## 3. Kill the obvious noise

Fans, fridges, traffic-side windows. You don't need a treated studio; you need thirty seconds of thinking about what's humming in the background.

## 4. Sing the whole phrase

Takes that trail off before the phrase ends are the most common thing VOX flags — and the easiest to fix. Decide where the line ends *before* you sing it, and hold your energy through it.

## 5. Record more than one take

Single takes lie. Three takes of the same phrase show you what's consistent and what was a one-off. Consistency is the thing worth practising.

None of this requires gear. It requires intent — which is rather the point.
