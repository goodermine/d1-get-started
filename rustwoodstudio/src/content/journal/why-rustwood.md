Rustwood Studio exists because of one stubborn observation: people don't get better at things by being told they're already great.

They get better through **practice, feedback, and the right systems** — the same way a blade gets its edge. Heat, pressure, repetition, correction. Forged, not found.

## What that means for voice

Most singers practise blind. They sing, it feels okay (or doesn't), and they move on. No evidence, no direction, no record of what changed.

The Rustwood approach is different:

1. **Learn the foundations** so you know what you're actually doing.
2. **Test against evidence** — a recording and honest metrics beat a feeling.
3. **Practise with direction**, not just repetition.
4. **Go deeper** when the foundations hold.

That's the whole studio in four lines. The book teaches the foundations. VOX provides the evidence. Coaching provides the direction. The rest is your reps.

## Why "Rustwood"

Rust is what happens to good steel that stops being used. Wood is what everything sturdy gets built on. The name is a reminder of both: capability decays without practice, and nothing lasts without a foundation.
